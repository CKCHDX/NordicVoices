# Resources for Native Speakers

## Welcome Native Speakers!

This project exists to preserve and celebrate Nordic dialects, and **you** are the most important resource we have. Your knowledge, pronunciation, and authentic usage are invaluable.

## Validation Guide

### How to Review Translations

When we ask you to review, we'll show you:

```
Swedish: Jag heter Anna och jag bor i Stockholm
Translation: Ja heit Anna å ja bor i Stockholm
```

Please tell us:

1. **Accuracy** (Is the meaning correct?)
   - ○ Perfect
   - ○ Mostly correct
   - ○ Partially correct
   - ○ Wrong

2. **Naturalness** (Would you actually say it this way?)
   - ○ Yes, exactly like this
   - ○ Yes, but maybe with small changes
   - ○ Awkward, but understandable
   - ○ No, we don't say it this way

3. **Your version** (How would YOU say it?)
   - Write it the way you would naturally say it

4. **Notes** (Any comments?)
   - Regional differences
   - Context matters (formal vs. casual)
   - Age-related variations

**Your Info** (optional):
- Name: _________________
- Age: _________________
- Where you learned the dialect: _________________
- Where you live now: _________________

### Example

**Word in Dialect**: gatt

**Swedish Translation**: måste

**English Meaning**: must (have to)

**How to Pronounce**: "gat" (hard G, short A sound)

**Example Sentence**: I gatt gå heim nu (I have to go home now)

**Notes**: Very common word, used by all ages in Västerbotten

## Audio Recording Guidelines

### What to Record

**Priority**:
1. Common greetings and phrases
2. Numbers 1-20
3. Days of the week, months
4. Example sentences from the translator
5. Short stories or conversations

### How to Record

**Equipment**:
- Smartphone voice recorder (perfectly fine!)
- Computer microphone
- Professional equipment (if you have it)

**Tips**:
- Find a quiet place
- Speak naturally (not too slow, not too fast)
- Say each phrase twice
- State the phrase number or Swedish original first

**Format**:
- MP3 (preferred)
- WAV
- M4A

### Where to Send

- **Email**: recordings@dialect-preservation.example
- **Cloud storage**: We can provide a link
- **YouTube/SoundCloud**: Share links with us

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: I'm not sure my dialect is "correct" enough

**A**: There is no "wrong" way to speak your dialect! We want authentic usage, not perfection. Regional and generational variations are all valuable and important to document.

### Q: My grandparents spoke the dialect, but I only know some words

**A**: That's perfectly okay! Even partial knowledge is valuable. Share what you know, and we'll mark it with appropriate context (e.g., "historical usage" or "remembered from grandparents").

### Q: I speak a mixture of dialect and standard Swedish

**A**: Most people do! Modern dialect speakers often code-switch. Tell us about your natural usage - that's real language data.

### Q: What if I disagree with something already in the project?

**A**: Please tell us! Dialects have variations, and multiple perspectives help us understand the full picture. Open an issue or contact us directly.

### Q: Can I bring my friends/family who also speak the dialect?

**A**: Absolutely! The more native speakers involved, the better. This is a community project.

### Q: Will this help preserve the dialect?

**A**: Yes! By documenting words, pronunciation, and usage patterns, we create resources that can be used by:
- Younger generations learning the dialect
- Researchers studying language change
- Community groups preserving heritage
- Teachers creating educational materials
- Anyone interested in learning

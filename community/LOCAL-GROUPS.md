# Local Groups & Organizations

## Community Organizations Supporting Nordic Dialect Preservation

---

## Introduction

This directory connects learners, speakers, and researchers with local organizations, museums, heritage societies, and community groups working to preserve Nordic dialects.

## Sweden

### National Organizations

#### ISOF - Institute for Language and Folklore
- **Website**: https://www.isof.se
- **Location**: Uppsala, Sweden
- **Focus**: Swedish dialects, folklore, language preservation
- **Resources**: 
  - Dialect recordings archive
  - Research publications
  - Educational materials
  - Language maps
- **Contact**: info@isof.se

#### Svenska Akademien (Swedish Academy)
- **Website**: https://www.svenskaakademien.se
- **Location**: Stockholm, Sweden
- **Focus**: Swedish language standardization and preservation
- **Resources**:
  - Svenska Akademiens Ordbok (SAOB) - comprehensive dictionary
  - Language advice
  - Historical documentation

## Estonia (Historical Swedish Communities)

### Estlandssvenska

**Eesti Rootsi Muuseum (Estonian Swedish Museum)**
- **Website**: https://www.ermuseum.se
- **Location**: Gamla Uppsala, Sweden (relocated from Estonia)
- **Focus**: Estlandssvenska (extinct but documented)
- **Resources**:
  - Historical records
  - Oral history recordings
  - Genealogical resources
  - Cultural artifacts

**Note**: Estonian Swedish communities were evacuated to Sweden in 1944. The dialect is extinct but well-documented.

## Heritage Societies (Hembygdsföreningar)

Almost every Swedish municipality has a hembygdsförening (heritage society) that preserves local dialect and culture.

### How to Find Your Local Heritage Society

1. **Visit**: [Sveriges Hembygdsförbund](https://www.hembygd.se)
2. **Search**: By municipality or region
3. **Contact**: Most have email or phone
4. **Visit**: Many have museums or meeting places open to public

### What They Offer

- Local dialect documentation
- Oral history recordings
- Cultural events and festivals
- Learning opportunities
- Connection to elderly speakers
- Historical archives

## How to Connect

### For Learners

1. **Contact local museum** in the region of your target dialect
2. **Join online communities** for discussion and practice
3. **Attend cultural events** to hear the dialect spoken
4. **Take courses** through folk high schools or universities
5. **Visit heritage societies** in the dialect region

### For Researchers

1. **Connect with ISOF** for archival access
2. **Contact university departments** for collaboration
3. **Reach out to local museums** for field work
4. **Join academic networks** in dialectology
5. **Attend conferences** on Scandinavian linguistics

### For Native Speakers

1. **Join your local hembygdsförening** to connect with other speakers
2. **Contact preservation projects** to contribute knowledge
3. **Participate in documentation** efforts (recordings, interviews)
4. **Teach younger generations** through schools or community programs
5. **Advocate** for dialect recognition and support

## Starting a Local Group

Want to start a dialect learning group in your area?

### Steps

1. **Identify interest**: Find 5-10 interested people
2. **Find a venue**: Library, community center, café
3. **Set a schedule**: Weekly or biweekly meetings
4. **Plan activities**:
   - Practice conversations
   - Listen to recordings
   - Read texts together
   - Invite native speakers
   - Watch films/videos
5. **Promote**: Social media, local bulletin boards
6. **Register** (optional): As a study circle for funding

### Resources Available

- Learning materials from this project
- Support from heritage organizations
- Potential funding through study associations
- Online tools and translators

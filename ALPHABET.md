## 📄 Stress & Intonation

### Word Stress
- **Primary stress** (ˈ): Falls on first syllable in most words
  - **kˈómpa** (friend) - stress on first syllable
  - **fˈårə** (colors) - stress on first syllable

- **Secondary stress** (ˌ): Compound words sometimes have secondary stress
  - **husʿmàjd** (house maid)

### Tone Accent (Musical Pitch)

Bondska uses two distinct tonal accents:

#### **Accent 1 (Acute/Single Peak)**
- Rising-falling pitch pattern
- Used in many monosyllabic words
- Examples: **k̩aː** (cat), **m̩aːn** (man)

#### **Accent 2 (Grave/Double Peak)**
- Rising-falling-rising pattern
- Used in compound and derived words
- Examples: **k̩̩aːtor** (cats), **m̩̩aːnnen** (the man)

## 🗙️ Bondska vs Standard Swedish Comparison

### Key Sound Differences

| Sound | Standard Swedish | Bondska | Example |
|-------|-----------------|---------|----------|
| **R** | Uvular [R] or alveolar [r] | **Retroflex** [̤r̤] | rö (red) |
| **Initial sj** | [ʃ] (sh) | [ʂ] (retroflex sh) | sjo (→ ski) - "sea" |
| **Initial kj** | [ç] (hue) | [kj] (kj) | kjol → kje:l (skirt) |
| **Final -d** | Often silent [t] | Pronounced [d] | ord [ɔrd] (word) |
| **Vowel length** | Often shortened | **Extended** | båt [båːt] (boat) |
| **-ER ending** | [ər] (schwa-r) | [ɛ] (more open) | kåter (cats) |

### Common Phonetic Shifts (Phase 2)

| Swedish | Bondska | Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|----------|
| /e/ | /ɛ/ | Vowel opening | se → se (see) |
| /o/ | /u/ | Vowel closing | bok → buk (book) |
| /ö/ | /ɔ/ | Vowel lowering | söck → søck (search) |
| /a/ | /å/ | Vowel rounding | kar → kår (beloved) |

## 💯 Pronunciation Guide by Letter

### How to Read This Guide

- **Bold** = Bondska letter
- [IPA] = International Phonetic Alphabet
- *italic* = English example
- BONDSKA = Bondska example

### Consonants Pronunciation

**B** [b] - *bed* - BONDSKA (dialect)
- Always like English "b"
- Voiced bilabial stop

**C** [k] - *car* - COLSKA (cold)
- Rare in Bondska (usually K)
- Pronounced like "k"

**D** [d] - *dog* - DÖ (you)
- Always like English "d"
- Voiced alveolar stop

**F** [f] - *far* - FRÅGOR (questions)
- Always like English "f"
- Unvoiced labiodental fricative

**G** [g] or [j] - GATA (go) or GÄ (go)
- Before A, O, U: like English "g"
- Before E, I, Y: like English "y" in "yes"

**H** [h] - *hat* - HÄR (here)
- Always like English "h"
- Glottal fricative

**J** [j] - *yes* - JA (yes)
- Always like English "y" in "yes"
- Palatal glide

**K** [k] - *king* - KÄ (cat)
- Always like English "k"
- Slightly aspirated in Bondska

**L** [l] - *love* - LÅG (law)
- Always like English "l"
- Alveolar lateral

**M** [m] - *man* - MAN (man)
- Always like English "m"
- Bilabial nasal

**N** [n] - *net* - NEJ (no)
- Always like English "n"
- Alveolar nasal

**P** [p] - *pen* - PRAST (priest)
- Always like English "p"
- Slightly aspirated

**R** [r̤] - *red* - RÖ (red)
- **KEY FEATURE**: Retroflex approximant
- Unique to Northern Swedish dialects
- Slightly guttural sound, deep in throat
- NOT uvular like in some Swedish regions

**S** [s] - *sun* - SNO (snow)
- Always like English "s"
- Alveolar fricative

**SK** [ʂ] - *shelf* - SKINA (shine)
- Retroflexed fricative (retroflex sh)
- Characteristic Bondska sound
- Deeper than Swedish "sj"

**T** [t] - *tip* - TA (take)
- Always like English "t"
- Alveolar stop, slightly aspirated

**TJ** [ç] or [ʈ] - HUG - TJOCK (thick)
- Palatal fricative
- Similar to German "ich" sound

**V** [v] - *van* - VI (we)
- Always like English "v"
- Labiodental fricative

**W** [v] - *van* - VA (water)
- Rare in Bondska
- Pronounced like "v"

### Vowels Pronunciation

**A** [aː] - *f**a**ther* - HAV (sea) - long
- Open front vowel
- Like "ah" sound
- Very clear in Bondska

**E** [eː] - *d**ay*** - EN (one) - long
- Close-mid front vowel
- Like "ay" without the "y"
- Pure monophthong (no diphthongization)

**I** [iː] - ***ee***d - NI (you plural) - long
- Close front vowel
- Like "ee" in "feet"

**O** [uː] - *b**oo**k* - MOOT (meeting) - long
- Close back vowel
- Lips rounded
- Like "oo" in "boot"

**U** [uː] - *b**oo**k* - HUS (house) - long
- Close back vowel
- Lips rounded
- Like "oo" in "boot"
- Very similar to O

**Y** [yː] - *F**ren**ch "tu"* - NY (new) - long
- Close front vowel, lips rounded
- Unique to Nordic languages
- Say "ee" then round lips

**Ä** [ɛː] - *str**a**nge* - LÄR (learn) - long
- Open-mid front vowel
- Between "a" and "e"
- More open than E

**Ö** [œː] - *F**o**r* - FÖR (for) - long
- Open-mid front vowel, lips rounded
- Between "a" and "o"
- Unique to Nordic languages

**Å** [åː] - *f**a**ther* but rounder - GÕ (go) - long
- Open back vowel, lips rounded
- Unique to Nordic languages
- Like "o" in "sport" with more open mouth

## 💶 Spelling Rules & Exceptions

### Consonant Doubling
- Double consonants indicate short vowel before them
- **kat** (one cat) vs **katt** (many cats?)
- Doubled consonants also used for emphasis or clarity

### Vowel Length Markers
- Long vowels: Single consonant after vowel
  - **ka** (cows) [kaː]
- Short vowels: Double consonant after vowel
  - **kaffe** (coffee) [kafé]

### Silent Letters
- **D** at end of words: Often pronounced in Bondska (unlike Swedish)
  - **ord** [uɔrd] (word) - D is heard
- **T** in some words: Sometimes silent
  - **det** [de] (it) - T may be silent

## 💽 Regional Variations

Bondska has regional sub-dialects:

### Coastal Bondska (Bohuslän)
- More open vowels
- Clearer consonants
- Used in urban areas

### Inland Bondska (Västerbotten)
- More vowel harmony
- Softer consonants in certain positions
- More archaic features

### Urban vs Rural
- **Urban**: Closer to Standard Swedish, faster tempo
- **Rural**: More distinctive features, slower, more melodic
